# Print files

> [!IMPORTANT]
> AI-assisted, not the official Silkscreen enclosure: see the notice at the top of the
> [main README](../README.md) and https://silkscreenreader.com/ai.

Everything to print for both Silkscreen cases. The files are generated by the case projects in
[`../enclosures/`](../enclosures/): don't edit them here, rebuild them (below). If you just want to print a case with
any printer, start at the [top-level README](../README.md#print-a-case): it points to the generic OrcaSlicer projects on
the website and says what each contains.

**Bottom keys and cranks, 2026-10-06: on a 2-layer raft** (Ian: "maybe add 2-layer rafts to the buttons/cranks.
That's what I've been doing so that they don't unstick from the build plate"). Both plate files now put every bottom
key and every crank (the coupons' keys too) on a 2-layer raft, set per object: *Raft layers* 2, *Raft contact Z
distance* 0.2 (one 0.2 layer of air between the raft and the part, so it lifts off; Orca's default is 0.1) and the
raft's *Initial layer expansion* 1 (Orca's default 2 runs neighbouring rafts on the plate into each other). Nothing
else changed: sliced from the plate files, each key is still exactly 50 layers (10.00 mm) above its raft, and the
keys' fit, the cranks' cheeks and bores and the no-supports checks read the same as without the raft. Peel the raft
off the key's -X end or the crank's pad end; a light scrape there is fine (up to 0.1 mm of residue is within the
crank spec), but don't sand the key's end, which sets its length. The key's bed-end chamfer stays: over the raft the
first layer is laid with ordinary lines and does not flare, but the chamfer takes nothing from the fit, keeps the
first layer inside the key's outline either way, and keeps the key right if it is printed without a raft.

**Bottom keys, both cases, 2026-10-12: a deeper well for each crank cheek** (Ian, from his printed and assembled
cases: "The crank hits a friction point when rotating from its button side to its switch side ... Perhaps the well
where this area sits can be made deeper?").
- **What it was.** Turned by hand in the open tub (face down, before the board is in), each crank's cheek rubbed the
  roof of its slot in the rail at about 40 degrees. The top corner of the cheek's loop passed under a roof that printed
  a little low. Face down, the crank and the rod sag through their play toward it.
- **The fix.** Each cheek slot's roof is higher: 0.45 mm on the 4.26 (z 3.60, was 3.15). On the 3.68 the slot now
  runs up to the skin (z 3.47; the 0.32 mm strip of rail over it is gone).
- **What to reprint.** Only the tub of a keyed case: on the 4.26 `4.26/tub-standard` or `4.26/tub-light-strip` (and
  every keyed configuration's tub), on the 3.68 `3.68/tub-standard` or `3.68/tub-light-strip`. The cranks, keys, rod,
  gate, lid and midplate are unchanged: keep the ones you have. The chinless tubs have no cranks and are unchanged.
- **In use** (board in, keys pressed) a 4.26 crank never reaches the rub: on the 4.26 the friction was only in
  assembly, turning a crank past about 20 degrees by hand, so a 4.26 case that works can stay as it is. The 3.68's
  in-use margin at the switch was thin too (down to about 0 at the worst stack-up), so reprint its tub.

**Your printed 4.26 case (test 2):** reprint **the tub you use (`tub-standard`; `tub-light-strip` is for the
unreleased light-strip case) and 4 `key-426`**. Both print with supports off, the keys on their -X end. The lid, midplate, cranks, gate and rod stay.

1. Open `silkscreen-426-plates.3mf` at plate 1 (standard tub).
2. Delete everything on it except the tub and the 4 keys.
3. Optional, first: `coupon-chin` with one `key-426` (plate 4, about 15 minutes) checks the hooks in their slots.
   It is a fit check only: nothing is chosen from it.
4. Assemble with the tub **face down, on a cloth, from the keys until the board is in**:
   - Drop each key in from inside, hooks in its two slots, and let it slide out until both hooks stop. It stands
     2.4 mm proud (2.5 before the 2026-10-03 keys) and level, and fits only one way round.
   - Then drop in the cranks (each rests on its key), and fit the rod, the gate, the board and the lid.
   - If the tub is turned face up before the board is in, the keys sink (or fall in, before the rod). Turn it face
     down again and they come back.
   - `4.26/ROD.txt` has the same steps.

In use, face up or held to read, each key rests on its switch as before. Face down, the keys drop 1.0 mm outward
onto their hooks, all level, and settle back when the case is turned over.

If your case is still the test-1 print, also reprint the 4 cranks (crank-M from plate 2 if your board has APEM
switches) and the lid. Ream the cranks' bores first.

Print from the plate file, not the loose STLs in `4.26/`. The plate file carries each part's own settings: Arachne
walls on the cranks, elephant-foot compensation 0 on the cranks, keys and gates, a 2-layer raft under every key and
crank (above), and the lid's side-key supports (see "Supports" below). A loose STL needs these set by hand.

- **No V at the parting line** (tub and lid): both halves are square.
- **Side pads 1.2 mm proud** (the lid, which carries the side keys; was 1.5): 0.80 / 0.70 at the click.
- **Plain pins on the midplate** (was snap posts): the PCBA sits on three pins (4.26: H1, H2, H6; 3.68: H2, H6) that locate it
  to ±0.155 mm; **it no longer clicks onto the midplate before the lid goes on, so keep the stack face down until the lid is
  on.** With the lid on nothing else changes.
Reprint `tub-*`, `lid` (`lid-chinless`) and `midplate` for your size and configuration; the keys, cranks, gate and rod are
unchanged. Both plate files and every configuration's project carry the new parts, and nothing else in them moved. Sliced in
the OrcaSlicer CLI with the CC2 and generic presets and read back from the G-code; not printed. (`../enclosures/case-426/README.md`,
"Emily B's edit"; the 3.68's section of the same name.)

**4.26 lid and midplate, 2026-10-24 (the same reprint: the bulge fix):** the 4.26 lid (`lid`, `lid-chinless`) and midplate that you
reprint for the 2026-10-23 changes also carry this, so there is nothing more to print. The lid's board posts H2, H5 and H6 stand one
layer (0.2) shorter, so the board rests on H1 and the lid's middle bows about 40 % less (a model estimate, not measured on a print;
H5's pin still reaches 0.8 mm into the board and still locates its top end). The midplate loses one screen spring, at the J6 header,
that printed as loose islands and pushed on nothing. The 3.68 is unchanged by this. If you already printed the 2026-10-23 files, the
lid and the midplate are the only parts that change; the tub, keys, cranks, gate and rod do not. Sliced in the OrcaSlicer CLI with
the CC2 and generic presets and read back from the G-code; not printed. (`../enclosures/case-426/README.md`, "The lid's bulge and
the board's posts".)

**Two-colour logo, a switch in the one file (2026-10-24; replaces the separate `-2colour` files of 2026-10-23):** the lid's two
back engravings (the wordmark and the book icon) can be filled with a second colour. Every lid, in both plate files and in
every per-configuration project, now carries the two bodies that fill them as parts that do nothing until you switch them on,
so the whole case stays on one plate and the logo prints as an empty engraving in one colour by default (OFF is the same job as
before: same layers, same time, same filament). The `silkscreen-426-plates-2colour.3mf` and `silkscreen-368-plates-2colour.3mf`
files and the `-2colour` per-configuration projects (`index-2colour.json`) are gone. The logo is the lid's first two layers, so
a manual filament swap cannot make it: you need a printer that changes filament by itself (a multi-material system or a tool
changer). To switch it on, in OrcaSlicer:

1. In the object list on the left, click the arrow beside the **lid** to expand it. Under it are the side-key support parts
   and two parts named `logo wordmark: ...` and `logo icon: ...`.
2. Click the first logo part, then Ctrl+click the second.
3. Right-click -> **Change type** -> **Part**. (OrcaSlicer 2.3.1 asks in a dialog and takes one part at a time: do each, pick Part.)
4. With both still selected, right-click -> **Change filament** -> **2** (or click each part's filament swatch in the list).
5. Set filament 2 to the logo's colour and filament 1 to the case's (the files preview them as a light colour and black).
6. Slice. Only the lid's first two layers use filament 2; the rest of the case, the lid's supports and the rafts stay filament 1.

The projects define both filaments and have **Prime tower off**, and a keyed plate cannot have one: OrcaSlicer refuses a prime
tower on a plate whose objects print over different numbers of raft layers ("The prime tower requires that all objects are
printed over the same number of raft layers"), and the keys and cranks sit on rafts. So the colour change uses your printer's
own purge: the Elegoo CC2 profile, an AMS-style system and a tool changer purge in the machine. Choose your printer's own
profile when you switch the logo on, because the generic Marlin profile in these files has no purge of its own. A printer that
can only purge into a prime tower (a Prusa MMU, for example) should first drag the lid onto an empty plate, then switch on
Others -> Prime tower for that plate; every plate's tower position is already set to a free spot (`wipe_tower_x`, `wipe_tower_y`).
On the chinless plate, which has no rafts, you can switch the tower on without moving the lid. The bodies are also single
files: `4.26/lid-inlay-wordmark`, `lid-inlay-icon` (and `lid-chinless-inlay-*`), `3.68/lid-inlay-*`, in the lid's own print frame.

### 4.26: the battery

The 4.26 case takes a 503035 LiPo (500 mAh; the case's model is 37.2 x 31.1 x 5.18 mm with its wrap and protection
board), held in the midplate's slot under its lip and snaps; a pack much thicker than 5.2 mm won't go under them.
Most sellers have it: Amazon US (AKZYTUE, Liter, Winfox, CKWJMUBS, LJHLR, ...), Adafruit #1578, Pimoroni.

- **Connector:** JST PH 2.0, 2 pins. Its polarity is **not standardised**: sellers wire it both ways, and one brand
  can ship both. Before the first plug-in, check with a meter that the red wire goes to the + pin of the battery connector
  (J5; the + is marked on the board's silkscreen). A PHR-2 housing can be re-pinned by lifting its two latches with a pin.
- **Protection board:** buy only a listing that states one (PCM, protection circuit). Some 503035 listings come with
  PH 1.25 or 3-pin leads by default; pick the 2-pin PH 2.0 option.
- **Charge voltage:** the board's charger stops at 4.2 V, so a "high-voltage" (4.35 V) cell is safe; it is only
  slightly undercharged.
- **Lead:** wind any spare lead round the midplate's two cleats.

### Supports

Only the lid gets supports, and only under its side keys (since 2026-09-30). Leave supports off for everything
else, and don't switch on automatic supports: the slicer would put them on the keys, cranks and gate, which don't
need them. The rafts under the keys and cranks are not supports: they are set per object, with supports still off.

- **Where:** each side key's hinge notch. For 3 mm from the key's root (where it joins the lid), the key's foot is
  cut away up to 1.05 mm above the bed, so the bar over it prints over air (3.4 mm on the 3.68 since 2026-10-12).
  Each notch gets a small grid, 4 layers tall, with one 0.2 mm layer of air under the key. The slicer runs each grid
  1-1.5 mm past the key's root, under the lid's rounded back edge beside the hinge (a pinhead mark there at most). Nothing goes under the lid's logo, its grip
  grooves or its screw holes, and nothing on the tub.
- **In the plate file** this is already set: the lid has supports on in manual mode (supports only inside a support
  enforcer) and one enforcer box per side key (5 on the 4.26, 3 on the 3.68; the 4.26 chinless lid on plate 3 (plates 4 and 5 of the CC2 file)
  has the same 5, and a 4.26 side-key layout's lid one per key it has, 3). Every other object has supports off.
- **After printing,** pick each grid out of its notch from outside the case with a fingernail or tweezers. It sits
  one 0.2 mm air layer under the bar, with no dense interface, and about 0.4 mm off the walls beside it. Checked with the
  OrcaSlicer CLI only: not printed yet, and not opened in ElegooSlicer.
- **From the STL instead:** in OrcaSlicer, on the lid only, set *Support* on, type *normal(manual)*, *on build plate
  only*, top Z distance 0.2, top interface layers 0, support/object XY distance 0.4, and the raft's initial layer
  expansion 0 (it also widens the support's first layer, into the slot behind the key). Then paint supports (or add
  a support enforcer) on the underside of each notch's roof only.

The screw holes need no supports: each head's counterbore steps to a slot and then a square over its ceiling, so
the two layers above the seat print as short bridges (since 2026-09-30).

### Rafts (bottom keys and cranks)

Every bottom key and every crank prints on a 2-layer raft (since 2026-10-06), so the small parts stay stuck to the
plate. The plate files set it per object; on a loose STL, in OrcaSlicer (or ElegooSlicer) right-click the object,
*Add settings* → *Support*, and set:

| Setting (OrcaSlicer) | Saved as | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raft layers | `raft_layers` | 2 | a 0.2 base layer and a 0.3 interface layer, its top 0.5 above the plate |
| Raft contact Z distance | `raft_contact_distance` | 0.2 | one 0.2 layer of air between the raft and the part, as under the lid's supports: it lifts off cleanly (Orca's default 0.1 bonds harder) |
| Raft expansion | `raft_expansion` | 1.5 (Orca's default, left as is) | the raft 1.5 mm wider all round than the part's end |
| Initial layer expansion (raft) | `raft_first_layer_expansion` | 1 | the raft's first layer 2.5 mm past the part's end; Orca's default 2 runs neighbouring rafts into each other at the plates' 6 mm spacing |

Keep elephant-foot compensation 0 (and Arachne walls on the cranks) as well. After printing, peel the raft off; a
light scrape of the key's -X end or the crank's pad end is fine, but don't sand the key's end: its length (10.00 mm,
50 layers) is its side play. Checked with the OrcaSlicer CLI on the plate files (each part reads as without the
raft, `slice_check.py`); not yet printed from these files, and not opened in ElegooSlicer.

## Any other 4.26 configuration: its own OrcaSlicer project

Each of the 32 configurations has an OrcaSlicer project of its own, `<slug>.3mf`, with what that case prints and
nothing else. **OrcaSlicer, any printer** (2026-10-11): these are the files the website is meant to serve, and they carry
OrcaSlicer's generic presets (Generic Marlin Printer 0.4 nozzle, 0.20mm Standard, Generic PLA), which every OrcaSlicer
install has, so they are not tied to one printer. Every plate fits a 220 x 210 mm bed (Ender-3 class 220 x 220,
Prusa MK4 250 x 210, Bambu 256 and anything larger).

- **Open it in OrcaSlicer, then pick your printer** in the printer box (and its 0.20 mm process and your PLA if Orca
  doesn't pick them). Keep a **0.4 mm nozzle and 0.2 mm layers** (first layer 0.2 too): the keys are exactly 50
  layers long, and the raft's air gap and the lid supports' gap are one layer.
- What the parts need rides on the parts, so a printer or process change keeps it: the rafts under the keys and
  cranks, the lid's side-key supports (supports only there), elephant-foot compensation 0 on the keys, cranks and
  gates, Arachne walls on the cranks, and no brim. Don't switch on automatic supports. The project also prints a
  one-loop skirt first (since 2026-10-13, a project setting, not a part's): if Orca asks on a printer change whether
  to keep the project's changed settings, keep them.
- **Other slicers are not supported.** PrusaSlicer, Cura and the rest don't read these per-part settings; use
  OrcaSlicer.

The two plate files above (`print/silkscreen-426-plates.3mf` and `silkscreen-368-plates.3mf`) are in git and
generic, like the per-configuration projects. The author's Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 versions of both
(`make_plates.py --printer cc2`, into `print/cc2-local/`) and of the per-configuration files (`make_plates.py configs
--printer cc2`, into `out/configs-cc2/`) are his own working copies: git-ignored, not published, and made by running
those two commands. The generic per-configuration files are not in git either: the website is meant to publish them
(as of 2026-10-21 it does not yet); you can also make yours with the case project (below). The website's builder is to offer the 16 standard and chinless ones (side keys as one four-way choice,
as below); the light-strip ones are in development and it will not offer them. The top-level
[README](../README.md#print-a-case) lists what each file contains.

**1. Pick your slug,** `base[-sides][-nodev]`:
- **base**, by your panel and your board's bottom row:

  | | bottom-row buttons fitted (SW9, SW8, SW3, SW2) | bottom-row buttons NOT fitted |
  |---|---|---|
  | GDEQ0426T82-FT01C (touch + frontlight) | `standard` | `chinless` |
  | plain GDEY0426T82 + the Silkscreen light strip (in development, not released) | `light-strip` | `light-strip-chinless` |

- **sides**, the page keys you want, as you hold it (screen toward you): none for all four; `-leftkeys` for the left
  pair only (up and down on the left edge: SW7, SW5); `-rightkeys` for the right pair only (up and down on the right
  edge: SW4, SW1); `-upperkeys` for the upper key on each side (SW7, SW4). The power button (SW10, top of the right
  edge) is on every case. A key left out is the plain
  wall; its switch may stay on the board (the case clears it and never presses it).
- **`-nodev`** if your board has no dev header (the 12-pin expansion header, labelled J6). A board WITH the header
  needs a case without `-nodev`.
- Examples: `standard`, `chinless-upperkeys`, `chinless-leftkeys-nodev`. The 32 are listed with what each
  needs on the board in [`../enclosures/case-426/README.md`](../enclosures/case-426/README.md#the-32-configurations).

**2. Make it.** From `enclosures/case-426`, with the case project's Python (`$py`,

```powershell
& $py -B src/pipeline.py --builds <slug>      # builds and checks it (and the twins its checks compare with)
```

The file is `enclosures/case-426/out/configs/<slug>.3mf` (the generic one), sliced and read back by the pipeline. A

**3. What is in it:** plate 1 "Case: <your configuration in words>": the tub and the lid (with one support enforcer
per side key it has) side by side, the midplate turned crosswise in front of them; with bottom keys also 4
`key-426`, 4 `crank-T` and 2 gates (one spare) beside the midplate and the rod jig beside the lid. With bottom keys,
plate 2 holds 4 `crank-M`, only for APEM MJTP1117 boards. There are no test prints: with bottom keys, print the gate
coupon from plate 4 of `silkscreen-426-plates.3mf` first (the plate file's step 1). About 41 g with bottom keys and
33 g chinless; the time is your printer profile's (the generic profile's cautious speeds give 3 h 20 min and 2 h 30
min, the CC2 1 h 40 min and 1 h 15 min). Checked with the OrcaSlicer CLI only (all 32 slice, a raft under every key
and crank, supports only in the lid's side-key enforcers): opened in the OrcaSlicer 2.4.0-dev GUI (2026-10-21) but not printed.
