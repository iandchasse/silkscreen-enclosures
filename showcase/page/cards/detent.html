The stack clicks in

Put the display, the midplate and the PCBA together and push them into the tub as one: the midplate clicks home at detents, and the stack stays there until the lid goes on.

Why. Until the lid was on, nothing held the stack: it slid in the tub, and nothing told you it was home. The two bottom screws go through the board beside the ribbons’ fold, so a stack sitting anywhere but home is the one way a screw could reach the glass or a ribbon.

How. At four places, the midplate’s edge is a spring strip: a 0.7 mm strip of the 1 mm plate, cut free by a mm slot behind it and across its tip, so it bends in the plate’s own plane. Near its tip a bump stands mm past the plate’s edge. The wall is only mm away, so on the way in the bump rides mm into it and the strip bends; then the bump snaps into a pocket cut mm into the wall, and that is the click. It prints like the rest of the midplate: flat, screen side down.

Where it stops. The pocket’s roof catches the bump when the plate is 0.15–0.55 mm short of home (thicker panels sit closer), so the stack is held within that, and a firm pull still takes it out. The pocket runs on toward the front, from z 1.40 to 3.35, enough for the panel’s tolerance, so once the lid and screws pull everything home the bumps sit free in their pockets and fight nothing: the springs, not the detents, still set where the panel sits.
