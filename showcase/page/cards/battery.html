Battery: a slot it clicks into, and a way out for the card

Battery: mAh in the lid, over the shallow patch of the board

The cell sits in a slot on the back of the midplate, which stands up through the board’s notch. A lip along its left edge and two snaps on its right hold it down, and spare lead winds on two cleats beside it. The slot’s bottom stop is low enough that the microSD card, which ejects straight at the cell, can get out once the cell is swung aside.

The case is as thin as what sits under the battery. Behind the board five things are deep, and everything else stands 1.6 mm or less, so the cell lies over that shallow middle and costs only its own thickness. It rides in the lid: plug it in with the lid stood up beside the top edge, then fold the lid down like a book.

The slot. The left rail stands off the midplate past the cell’s back and turns in over its edge: a lip mm over the cell, all along, mm off its back. On the right, the two posts either side of the lead’s exit are snaps: each ends in a 45° barb mm over the cell’s other edge, on a post thin enough to flex out of the way. Tuck the cell’s left edge under the lip and press the right side down, and it clicks in; or, with the midplate out of the tub, slide it in from the midplate’s top edge, where the lip is flared. Once the midplate is in, the tub’s top wall closes the slot above the cell, and the lid’s two springs press it lightly forward. Every lip’s and barb’s underside is 45°, so the midplate still prints screen side down with no supports.

The cleats. The first and last right-hand posts stand past the cell’s back with caps along their length. A real pack’s lead is longer than the run from its exit to the battery connector (J5), so wind the spare round the two cleats, 18.7 mm apart, or over the cell’s right edge, where the lid’s springs don’t reach. They are on the midplate, not the lid, so the lead stays put when the lid comes off.

The microSD card. The card socket (J7) is a push-push socket on the back of the board whose mouth faces the cell’s bottom edge from 3.4 mm away, so the cell has to move before a card can come out. The bottom stop is only mm tall and sits in front of the board’s rear face, clear of where the card travels. With the lid off, unwind the lead from the cleats, lift the cell’s right side past its two snaps and swing it out from under the lip (it stays on its lead), push the card, and it ejects and pulls free.

The pack is the site’s 503035 model (cell, wrap and protection board), 37.20 × 31.10 × 5.18 mm. Measure the real pack before printing: the lip and snaps sit mm off the model’s back face, so a pack much thicker than 5.2 mm will not go under them.

Why here. Behind the board only five things are deep: battery J5 4.9 mm. Under the bay the deepest part is the microSD socket J7 ( mm). The bay runs up past the board’s trimmed edge next to the battery connector, so the lead is a short run to the plug. Its bottom-left corner comes within mm of the antenna end’s inner corner (hatched): not over it, but close, so check Wi-Fi range on the first build.

Retention. The pack’s bottom edge tucks under a lip that reaches mm over it, and the cell is taped down; nothing on the lid presses it (there were two hooks until 2026-10-19: they pressed the cell and its lead). Push the pack to the right, and down only until it stops under the lip. A low wall across the top end stops it sliding out from under the lip, which keeps at least mm over it. The pocket takes a cell up to mm thick (the datasheet’s 5.3 mm and two layers of tape on each face) and leaves mm of air under the microSD socket (J7) as printed.

Lead.

Service. For the microSD card or the battery: screws out, lift the lid from the bottom edge and fold it back toward the top, unplug the battery. The pack stays in the lid; peel its tape to take it out.
