USB-C: the divot, floored by the tub

A recess for the plug’s overmold, whose floor is the tub itself: a mm wall round the port, with a hole that only shows the port. There is no separate bezel part.

out 3.6 mm

A mated plug’s overmold sits mm in front of the receptacle, so whatever surrounds the port can stand only that far in front of the socket’s mouth before it starts to hold a plug back. The case face is 2.9 mm out, so the port is a recess — the divot — sized for typical cables ( mm), with a 45° lead-in at the case face.

Its floor is the tub. Rev F–H had a printed bezel that had to stay put on the connector by itself during assembly, and it would not stay on the dummy’s USB-C socket. The catch is where the floor can go. The connector’s mouth sits right at the panel’s edge, and the panel passes there on its way in, so the tub can reach no closer than the panel’s corridor, 0.115 mm in front of the mouth. A mm floor — about the thinnest wall a 0.4 mm nozzle lays as one line — puts its face 0.61 mm in front of the mouth. A typical plug therefore seats to within about 0.16 mm of full depth. Try your cables on the dummy.

The hole in the floor is the connector’s opening plus 0.1 mm a side, so the plug passes and nothing else of the board shows. It runs a little further toward the board than that (a few tenths of a millimetre, to a layer line), so a plug still clears it if the connector seats nearer the board than drawn, as board Rev 1.02’s does. Below the hole the floor is open to the parting line — a notch — because the tub prints face down and the hole’s lower edge would otherwise be a bridge. A block on the lid rises into the notch and closes it, just in front of the connector’s shell.
