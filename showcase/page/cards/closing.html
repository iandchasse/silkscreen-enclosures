Closing up: three screws, a board that sits on pins, and a printed gate for the rod

The tongue and the latches line the halves up and hold them. The board is the datum the keys and buttons work from, so it sits on the midplate’s pins at three points before the lid is anywhere near it. Three screws then close the case, and the lid’s rim closes over the small printed gate in the chin’s left wall that holds the bottom keys’ rod.

The screws. All three are M2 × 10 socket head caps (ISO 4762). They thread-form into their pilots, and their heads sit in counterbores. The bottom pair, at mounting holes H3 and H4, clamp the board between a lid boss and a tub boss.The bottom pair sit in the chin’s corners, below the rod, each through a post in the lid into a block of the tub that is solid from the parting plane to the pilot’s end; the top pair stand in the top corners. None goes through the board: the lid’s posts at H3 and H4 carry a pin into the board’s hole instead, the board’s X/Y stop at its bottom edge.

The board sits on pins first. The midplate carries a plain pin through mounting holes H1, H2 and H6. Each is a mm pin in the board’s mm hole, its tip rounded and reaching 1.0 mm past the board’s rear face into a bore in the lid’s post, so the board is located to mm a side in X and Y. Nothing clicks: until the lid is on, nothing holds the board in Z, so the stack stays face down. (Until 2026-10-23 these were two-finger snap posts with a barb that caught the board; Ian took a community member’s plain pin instead.)

With the lid on, the board sits on the lid’s posts and the pins only locate it, never clamp it. That matters: the midplate floats with the panel’s thickness, and anything that clamped the board to it would bow the board or crush the glass. Mounting hole H5 keeps its locating pin and sets X and Y at the top end. Mounting hole H1 went with the board’s cut, so a post in the empty site of the left lower page key (SW5) carries the board’s top-left corner instead.

The rod’s gate. The bottom keys’ cranks turn on a mm length of mm filament, pushed in along a channel in a slot in the chin’s left wall and through a mm hole. A small printed gate then drops into that slot from the open back: its flange sits behind two lips of the wall, its bevel pushes the rod the last 1.75 mm home, and its flat inner face stops the rod’s square tail, so the rod has mm of end play and cannot walk out. The lid’s rim closes mm over the gate’s top. A straightened paperclip in its mm pull hole lifts it out, and the rod then pushes out to the left; that is how the cranks come out again. No screw.
